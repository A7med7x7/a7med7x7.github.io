for the past several months, I have been unusually active on social media, and for several reasons. one of them is that I have been conducting a little experiment of my own, observing how people react to different kinds of posts I share, or how they respond when I deliberately take an artificial position/stand and argue as though I genuinely believe in it. I have been interested in the nature of these interactions, what makes people agree, what makes them defensive, and what makes a conversation turn into an argument. the concept of a social network has never particularly fascinated me -even if at someone it did it is long ago that is faded - at least not in the way it seems to fascinate others. I have never had a hunger for attention from my network, nor have I ever felt the need to build an audience simply for the sake of having one. in fact, I am someone with quite a few walls around them. I feel loved and content with the relationships I already have with my family and friends, and that love is all I have ever really tried to find and allow myself to experience fully.

that said, I am still human, and I love expressing myself. I love putting into words the things that matter to me, sharing the things I make, and allowing people to see a little of how I think. I love meeting people, including those I have known of for years but never really got the chance to know, and those whom life happens to bring my way. and I believe this willingness to put myself out there has yielded something quite beautiful. as when I had the opportunity to share my craft across different fields, I encounter people I would otherwise never have met, and build some genuinely wonderful friendships, both online and in real life. perhaps there is something to be said about making your work visible, not because you are desperately looking for an audience, but because sometimes the people who are meant to find you need a way to do so.

> No matter how isolated you are and how lonely you feel, if you do your work truly and conscientiously, unknown friends will come and seek you.
> 
> — Carl Jung

but as time goes by, and as I grow older, I find myself [changing](https://a7med7x7.github.io/posts/i-fear-the-person-i-will-become/) at an unusually alarming pace. sometimes I feel that the community I grew up in is simply not keeping up with the person I am becoming. this creates a distance between me and the people, customs, and ways of thinking that once felt familiar. it is not necessarily that I have stopped loving these people, or that everything I grew up around has suddenly become meaningless. rather, I have started questioning things I may for once accepted without much thought. I question the culture of conformity, the tendency to inherit beliefs without examining them, and whether some of the things we collectively defend are actually doing any good for our well being, our humanity, or even the greater purpose they were originally meant to serve.

and once you begin allowing [complexity](https://a7med7x7.github.io/posts/allow-yourself-to-get-lost/) to exist, instead of constantly shutting doors through rigid labels and premature judgments, you begin to see how much of the world refuses to fit into the categories you were given. you start noticing contradictions, competing truths, and circumstances that cannot be reduced to a simple right or wrong. suddenly, so many things become [suboptimal](https://a7med7x7.github.io/posts/not-broken-just-suboptimal/) for you to fit into. and don't get me wrong, it is not necessarily because they are entirely wrong, but because they no longer accommodate the way you have learned to see the world.

and when you get caught up in this constant process of questioning, in your own commitments and ambitions, and in the rather exhausting business of confronting your own shortcomings, something else begins to happen. you look around and realize that there are very few people, if any, with whom you can genuinely enjoy the experience of being alive. people with whom you can have a conversation without having to perform a version of yourself that makes the interaction socially acceptable. people with whom you can explore an idea without worrying about where the conversation might lead, or whether an honest disagreement will somehow become a personal offense. you begin to realize that what you miss is not necessarily company, but the freedom to exist in someone's company without having to constantly negotiate your own thoughts.

> As a child I felt myself to be alone, and I am still, because I know things and must hint at things which others apparently know nothing of, and for the most part do not want to know.
> 
> — Carl Jung

what I have come to understand is that very few people seem willing to tolerate complexity for very long. everyone is looking for answers, preferably quick ones, and sometimes [answers](https://goodthoughts.page/q/questions-that-cant-be-answered/) that cannot themselves be questioned. but I also understand that people have other needs, and perhaps those needs are far more fundamental than the intellectual curiosity I keep looking for. the need to be seen, appreciated, loved, reassured, and to feel that they matter to someone. or the feeling of power and authority, when these needs go unmet, they can create a state of imbalance in someone's life, one that sometimes manifests as hostility, resentment, or even toxicity toward the people closest to them, and it is always that case without the person recognizing what is happening. 

there is something particularly difficult about these hidden needs. they are primitive, deeply human, and rarely admitted in their actual form. just like a [shadow](https://www.philippejacquet.co.uk/shadow-work/?utm_source=chatgpt.com#:~:text=because%20the%20shadow%20is%20defined%20as%20the%20part%20you%20have%20arranged%20not%20to%20see.%20Introspection%20tends%20to%20circle%20it.), its never meant to be explored, instead, people find ways to rationalize them, disguise them, or express them through entirely different behaviors. someone might spend years [trying to become more successful](https://a7med7x7.github.io/posts/collecting-medals-subconsciously/), more desirable, more accomplished, or more agreeable, without ever confronting the wound that makes them feel insufficient in the first place. they [keep running](https://a7med7x7.github.io/posts/it-grows-teeth/#:~:text=and%20you%20cannot%20outrun%20something%20that%20lives%20in%20your%20chest) in circles, treating the symptoms while leaving the underlying causes untouched. and because the source of the imbalance remains, the imbalance inevitably returns. sometimes what appears to be a personality problem is a much older unmet need asking to be acknowledged.

> We silence headaches but not the sorrows that seed them. We knead stiff muscles but not the ghosts that knot them.

and perhaps this is where my own difficulties with people begin. [recent attempts](https://a7med7x7.github.io/posts/off-my-chest-to-my-friends/) have made it increasingly difficult for me to continue helping the people around me. I have spent a considerable amount of time trying to be there for my friends and everyone, trying to understand, accommodate, encourage, and sometimes carry people through circumstances they struggle to navigate themselves. it becomes particularly exhausting when you begin noticing that the people you are trying so hard to help are not putting even a fraction of that effort into helping themselves.

I remember an observation Jordan Peterson makes in _12 Rules for Life_ that a friend sent me, about the strange contradictions in how people distribute their care. he describes how some patients struggle to follow their own medical prescriptions, even in serious circumstances, while being remarkably attentive when it comes to administering medication to their sick pets. there is something revealing about this contrast. people can extend extraordinary patience, tenderness, and responsibility toward another living creature while struggling to offer themselves even the most basic forms of care. sometimes the problem is not that people do not know how to care. it is that somewhere along the way, they have developed a relationship with themselves in which they no longer believe they are equally deserving of that care.

I find this particularly difficult to witness in people I love. because I can see what they could do for themselves, sometimes I can even see the possibilities they refuse to acknowledge, and naturally, I want to help them get there. but wanting something for someone does not mean you can make it happen for them. you can offer a hand, [point toward a door](https://a7med7x7.github.io/posts/about-the-way-forward/), and perhaps even walk alongside someone for a while, but you cannot make the decision to walk on their behalf. eventually, you begin exhausting yourself trying to compensate for someone's unwillingness or inability to participate in their own life.

and I have spent years trying to learn that [this is not my problem to fix](https://a7med7x7.github.io/posts/before-you-heal-someone/). the same way I insist that people must learn to let go of the responsibility of fixing other people's shortcomings, wounds, and struggles beyond their comprehension, I must learn to apply that principle to myself. I cannot keep making exceptions simply because I happen to care. I cannot keep returning to the same position every time someone I love is struggling, convincing myself that perhaps this time I can make a difference. there is a difference between loving someone and assuming responsibility for their well being. and perhaps the hardest lesson for someone who has spent so much of their life caring for others is learning that these two things do not have to come together.

I might have to stand against my own loving and caring heart sometimes. I need to learn for the 100 times, that my capacity to care is not an unlimited resource, and that spending it everywhere leaves very little of it for the people and things that genuinely deserve my attention.

I am writing all of this because I want to make explicit what I have been feeling toward the people around me, my community, and the people I have come to know throughout these years. I know that many of them will probably never read this. perhaps that is part of the problem itself. we have become a community that produces very little of what it could, and reads even less of what is being produced. we are remarkably comfortable sharing opinions, reacting to things, and participating in the endless exchange of information, yet much less inclined to sit with someone's thoughts long enough to understand them. and if someone chooses not to read, not to understand, or not to make an effort to meet me halfway, I cannot keep treating that as a responsibility I have failed to fulfill. it is not always my responsibility to make myself understood.

every second I spend catching up with someone, maintaining a conversation, or participating in an interaction that I no longer have the capacity for, or that I know is unlikely to yield anything meaningful, is a second I could have spent giving something to the world. this might sound unnecessarily transactional, but I think we rarely acknowledge how much of our lives we surrender to social obligations, how much time we spend simply because we have learned that being available is what makes us good friends, good relatives, or good members of a community. and for the longest time, I blamed myself for not being available enough, for not showing up as often as people might have expected me to. luckily, a few of my friends never confused my availability with the amount of love I have for them. and I no longer want to measure my affection for people by how consistently accessible I am to them.

I also want to acknowledge that I have not always been on the receiving end of these difficulties. I have been ignorant, I have been difficult to deal with, and I have caused trouble for people who genuinely loved me. I have made mistakes, and I do not intend to present myself as someone who has figured everything out or somehow risen above the very things I am describing. one of my own shortcomings has always been [disappearing](https://a7med7x7.github.io/posts/learning-the-truth-about-how-i-love/) without telling people that I was going to do so. for a long time, I did not even recognize this as something I was consciously choosing. it was almost entirely subconscious, a way of withdrawing that I had never really examined. but this is perhaps the first time I am acknowledging it consciously, and I do not want to pretend that it has not affected people who cared about me.

I have given a great deal to the people around me, including many whose names I never even knew. I have never expected much in return. wishing me well has always been enough, and I have never needed people to repay every kindness or remember every little thing I have done. but I am asking for something now, perhaps something I should have asked for much earlier. I am asking people not to resent me for wanting more space in my own life. I am asking them to understand that my absence is not necessarily an expression of resentment, disappointment, or a lack of affection. sometimes it is simply a person choosing where their time, attention, and energy should go.

for the next few years, I want to return to spending my time on the things I love, the things that have always made me feel most like myself. I want to give a greater portion of my life to my work, my interests, my ambitions, and the people I genuinely love. my family, and those who may not share my blood but have become family in every sense that matters. I want to experience things without constantly feeling the need to report back, explain myself, or remain available to everyone who happens to know me.

I may still be around from time to time. I may share something, publish something, or occasionally participate in conversations on the internet. but there will be many times when I simply will not feel like responding, catching up, or maintaining a conversation. and I hope people can allow that to be what it is, without turning it into something personal. we all have our reasons for the ways we navigate life, and I am finally allowing myself to have mine.

salam.
