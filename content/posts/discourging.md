Someone told me something today, in the form of a notice. that I worry too much, that I keep looking at what could have been done better, analyzing everything, always looking ahead, and not living enough in the present. not stopping to appreciate how far someone has come, or how good the result already is. it's funny, because I had almost this exact conversation with [Alnour Alharin](https://alnouralharin.com/), I don't know, around seven months ago or something.

Alnour told me that his analyst mind is always searching for room for improvement, and that this doesn't usually work with social norms. people want a genuine connection. they want feedback on themselves too, not only on the material they are working on. its a bias we both carry, that we see every possible way a system could break and then try to perfect it.just like how engineers call this mapping the [failure modes](https://a7med7x7.github.io/series/notes-on-doing/page/2/), and it is a good habit when the thing in front of you is a system. with people it turns into overanalyzing.

Both of us, from the inside, are deeply passionate about progress, and genuinely happy when people make it. but part of us is dysfunctional in showing that. there is a real form of happiness inside for someone who accomplished a task, we just don't show it as often. and maybe sometimes we don't show it at all, which is exactly where the dysfunction is.

I think that in a world where we trained ourselves to always know what should be done next, we brought the same habit into our relationships. we don't stop enough to look at what is already good. I want to worry less about the part where this could break, or where it is a hard and complex problem to target, and to let people dream big, and to encourage them.

This opened my mind to one of my weakest points in social interactions. I sometimes discourage people by being too cautious, and the sometimes this caution comes from being too aware of the situation, of every way it could go wrong. the result is that I underestimate someone's ability to understand something/ navigate it, or I overestimate it. I will find a way to work around this. and I'm thankful for my best friend North, who saw this in me and told me directly.
